# The Natural World
## Seasons
There are familiar periods of high and low heat and activity by the great light that follow 365 days and a warm and cold season. Notably, though, the solstices both have a 10ish day period where the light stays dark during the day, and the world goes cold. Leading up to this, both in winter and summer, there is another 10ish day period where the light and chaos gets more and more intense until the first day of the solstice when things go dark all of a sudden. 

## Stars
There aren’t stars as we know them, but there are these aerostatic creatures who capture light during the day and release some throughout the night to communicate and impress each other. They took to the skies initially to escape predatory beasties in the swamp, only to find predatory birds. Eventually they grew their gas sacks so large, they carefully sit at the edge of poison gas that looms over the world. The light they produce is like that of a lavender neon light, inside their gas bodies. The larger they are, the brighter. The stars, as they’re simply called, seem to have an endless lifespan, as any intelligent cultures have known them to have always been there. Their movement and reproduction is so slow, nobody has been able to research it. Their existence as living creatures is common knowledge, but only the wizards atop the great tree have seen them with their naked eyes, and just barely at that. They seem to withstand some amount of poison gas, as any researchers turn back before fear of the poison. They are reliable, and people have always used them for navigation. 

## Sky and poison gas
The sky glows an atmospheric blue during the day, and is black at night. It is made up of a poison gas that’s formed directly from quantrite. It’s unable to decay or transmute into anything as far as people know. It eats away like an acid at anything that gets near it. During ‘sunrise and sunset’, the gas turns all colors as it starts to ‘ignite’ from the start of the great light. Waves of color slowly move across the sky from the center of the world, as the glow begins for the day.

Through the gas, it’s possible to see the lower edges of the sphere of the world faintly during the day, but the better view of the far sky is at night. Made smooth by the gas, are barely visible walls in a perfect sphere all around. Only after your eyes are well adjusted, and you have good low light vision do the polished facets and cracks start to appear far in the distance.
